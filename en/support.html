Support and FAQ

Last updated: August 30, 2026

System requirements

RoutineGuard requires macOS 14 or later and is available only for Mac.

First-time setup

  1. Save 1 to 8 routine items and choose minutes and seconds for each item.
  2. Choose the daily time when Mac protection begins. The default is 7:00 AM.
  3. Create a recovery code and store it safely away from your Mac.
  4. Complete the full-screen safety test, which deliberately does not block app switching.
  5. Enable “Open at Login,” then enable daily protection.

Daily protection cannot be enabled until you confirm that the recovery code is stored safely.

Full-screen protection and timers

At the chosen time, RoutineGuard presents its routine surface on every connected display. After the routine starts, your Mac manages each item duration and the total duration. The completion action is unavailable until every item timer has elapsed.

Protection does not disappear automatically when the timer ends. Choose “Complete and Use Mac” when completion becomes available to return to your regular Mac workspace.

Control from a phone

Connect the Mac and phone to the same local network, then open the pairing URL shown on the RoutineGuard waiting screen. Your Mac always remains the authority for starting and completing a routine.

  • Allow RoutineGuard to access the local network in macOS settings.
  • Guest Wi-Fi and networks that isolate devices may prevent the connection.
  • Do not share the pairing URL; it contains a private access token.
  • RoutineGuard does not provide cloud remote control or restrict apps on the iPhone itself.

If something goes wrong

Open “Having Trouble?” on the protection screen and enter your saved recovery code. After a 30-second delay that prevents accidental use, recovery dismisses today's protection and disables future automatic protection.

RoutineGuard is not an operating-system security boundary. It cannot completely prevent bypasses involving an administrator account, Safe Mode, Recovery Mode, power interruption, or other system-level actions.

If the Mac was asleep or powered off

RoutineGuard does not start or wake your Mac. If the Mac was unavailable at the chosen time, RoutineGuard checks the schedule on the next launch or wake and presents protection when required.

Stop protection or uninstall

While waiting, choose “Stop Protection” and disable “Open at Login” before deleting the app. During protection, complete the routine normally or use recovery first.

See the Privacy Policy for local-data deletion instructions.

Contact

Include your macOS version, RoutineGuard version, and the screen where the problem occurred, then email sunagaku.dx@gmail.com. Never send your recovery code or pairing URL.
